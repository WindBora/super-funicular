\begin{abstract}
We present a full-wave numerical study of plane-wave E-wave (TM)
scattering by two-dimensional open sinusoidal perfectly-electric-conductor
(PEC) strips.
The weighted SIE is discretized by a Chebyshev-Nystrom form of the
method of discrete singularities (MDS), after analytically extracting
the inverse-square-root edge behavior of the current.
A flat-strip benchmark provides a consistency check: boundary residuals
are near $10^{-13}$, while the total scattering width reaches
40.0 wavelengths at $N=200$.
For sinusoidal corrugation the reflected far-field develops finite-strip
lobes near the backward infinite-periodic Floquet reference angles.
A parametric study over amplitudes from 0.5 to 2.5 wavelengths and
spatial frequencies from 0.10 to 0.30 inverse wavelengths shows that
amplitude controls the relative strength of off-normal lobes while
spatial frequency sets their angular positions.
Across the 25 sinusoidal cases, the total scattering width spans
38.940--40.656 wavelengths (a $4.3\%$ relative range about the mean),
so corrugation changes both angular distribution and total scattering.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
method of discrete singularities, sinusoidal open strip, plane-wave

\section{Introduction}

Corrugated metallic surfaces are ubiquitous in microwave engineering.
The sinusoidal open strip is a particularly compact example: it serves
as a meander-line delay element~\cite{wadell1991}, slow-wave resonator,
and unit cell of high-temperature superconducting (HTS) bandpass
filters~\cite{lancaster1997,pozar2011}.
At microwave frequencies, HTS films satisfy $k\lambda_L\lesssim10^{-4}$
(London penetration depth $\lambda_L\!\sim\!100$\,nm), so the PEC
boundary condition is an excellent approximation.

When a plane wave is incident on such a strip, the corrugated surface
couples the specularly reflected field into additional off-normal lobes
at angles set by the sinusoidal period.
Here a \emph{lobe} means a local maximum of the angular far-field
amplitude.
The corresponding infinite periodic continuation is described by Floquet
theory~\cite{collin1991,balanis2012}, but a finite strip with few periods
has significant endpoint diffraction that demands a full-wave treatment.

Among rigorous 2-D methods for open-arc scattering, the classical
\emph{method of discrete singularities}
(MDS)~\cite{nosich2007,gandel2011} combines analytical edge-singularity
extraction with Chebyshev Nystrom discretization.
The implementation used here follows the differentiated MDS system: the
current endpoint singularity is removed analytically, the first-kind SIE
is differentiated into a Cauchy-type equation, and one supplementary
integral condition restores the lost constant.
The resulting $N\!\times\!N$ dense system is solved by Gaussian
elimination; the reported residuals measure algebraic satisfaction of
this discrete system.
MDS has been validated for parabolic multireflector antennas with
complex-source-point (CSP) beams~\cite{nosich2007,nosich_josa2007};
related open-strip and reflector formulations have also been treated
with analytical regularization, edge-conditioned Nystrom schemes, and
rapidly convergent SIE solvers~\cite{oguzer1995,oguzer2001,tong2006,
tsalamengas2006}. Here we apply the same open-arc framework to
plane-wave excitation and the finite sinusoidal strip geometry.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Boundary-value problem and SIE}

We seek the scattered E-wave phasor $U$ when a 2-D PEC arc $\Gamma$
is illuminated by a unit-amplitude plane wave. The total phasor is
$U_{\rm tot}=U_0+U$, and the corresponding physical field is
\begin{equation}
  u_{\rm tot}(\mathbf r,t)
  =\Re\!\left\{U_{\rm tot}(\mathbf r)e^{-i\omega t}\right\}.
  \label{eq:timeconvention}
\end{equation}
Thus the common Euler factor $e^{-i\omega t}$ is suppressed below. A
wave propagating along $\widehat{\mathbf d}=(\cos\beta,\sin\beta)$ has
the incident phasor
\begin{equation}
  U_0(\mathbf{r})
  = e^{ik(x\cos\beta + y\sin\beta)},
  \label{eq:planewave}
\end{equation}
with $k=2\pi/\lambda$, all lengths in wavelengths ($\lambda=1$, $k=2\pi$),
and $\beta=\pi/2$ (normal incidence, wave propagating in the $+y$
direction).
The scattered field satisfies the Helmholtz equation outside $\Gamma$,
the outgoing Sommerfeld condition
$\sqrt r\,(\partial U/\partial r-ikU)\to0$, local energy finiteness at
arc endpoints, and the Dirichlet condition $U|_\Gamma=-U_0|_\Gamma$.

The single-layer representation of $U$ leads, after parameterizing the arc
as $\mathbf{r}(t)=(x(t),y(t))$, $t\in[-1,1]$, and factoring the
inverse-square-root edge singularity into a smooth density $v(t)$
via $j\,|\mathbf{r}'(t)|=v(t)(1-t^2)^{-1/2}$, to the first-kind SIE
\begin{equation}
  \int_{-1}^{1}
  H_0^{(1)}\!\left(kR(t,t_0)\right)v(t)\,
  \frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}
  = -U_0(t_0),
  \label{eq:sie}
\end{equation}
where $R(t,t_0)=|\mathbf{r}(t)-\mathbf{r}(t_0)|$.
For the adopted $e^{-i\omega t}$ convention, $H_0^{(1)}(kR)$ is the
outgoing cylindrical-wave kernel.

\subsection{MDS discretization, geometry, and Floquet references}

After differentiation, the MDS system is discretized by weighted
Chebyshev quadrature plus one supplementary row. With source nodes
$t_i=\cos[(2i-1)\pi/(2n)]$, $i=1,\dots,n$, and second-kind collocation
nodes $\tau_j=\cos[j\pi/n]$, $j=1,\dots,n-1$, the boundary rows are
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
  \left[\frac{1}{t_i-\tau_j}+K(t_i,\tau_j)\right]v(t_i)
  = f(\tau_j),
  \label{eq:mds}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the regularized derivative kernel and, for
\eqref{eq:planewave},
\begin{equation}
  f(\tau)=-\frac{dU_0}{d\tau}
  =-ikU_0\!\left(\mathbf r(\tau)\right)
  \widehat{\mathbf d}\mathbin{\cdot}\mathbf r'(\tau).
  \label{eq:rhs}
\end{equation}
For one strip the regularized kernel is evaluated as
\begin{equation}
  K(t,\tau)=
  -kH_1^{(1)}(kR)\frac{\partial R}{\partial\tau}
  -\frac{1}{t-\tau},
  \label{eq:kreg}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial R}{\partial\tau}
  =-\frac{[\mathbf{r}(t)-\mathbf{r}(\tau)]
  \cdot\mathbf{r}'(\tau)}{R(t,\tau)} .
  \label{eq:drdtau}
\end{equation}
Thus the singular part is handled analytically, while only a smooth
remainder is sampled numerically.
The supplementary row is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}M(t_i)v(t_i)=c,
  \label{eq:supplement}
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the weighted single-layer integral of the kernel and
$c=-\int_{-1}^{1}U_0(\tau)d\tau/\sqrt{1-\tau^2}$.
For self interaction the logarithmic term in $M$ is also extracted:
\begin{equation}
  M(t_i)=\!\int_{-1}^{1}\!
  \left[H_0^{(1)}(kR)-\frac{2i}{\pi}\ln|t_i-\tau|\right]
  \frac{d\tau}{\sqrt{1-\tau^2}}-2i\ln2 .
  \label{eq:mself}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:mds}--\eqref{eq:supplement} are solved by Gaussian
elimination.
The scattered far-field pattern is evaluated from the asymptotic
single-layer potential.  The negative spatial sign below comes from the
large-distance expansion of $H_0^{(1)}(k|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|)$ and is
therefore consistent with the positive sign in~\eqref{eq:planewave}:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{\rm sc}(\varphi)
  \approx
  \frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi i}}
  \sum_{i=1}^{n}
  e^{-ik[\cos\varphi\,x(t_i)+\sin\varphi\,y(t_i)]}v(t_i),
  \label{eq:farfield}
\end{equation}
and the total 2-D scattering width is
\begin{equation}
  \sigma
  = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
    \bigl|\Phi_{\rm sc}(\varphi)\bigr|^2\,d\varphi.
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
This value follows the far-field normalization in~\eqref{eq:farfield}
and is used here for relative comparison across the same numerical
setup.
The residual reported below is
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{\rm bc}=\|A\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{b}\|_{\infty},
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
where $A\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{b}$ denotes the assembled discrete MDS
system.

The sinusoidal strip with $L=10\lambda$ and phase zero is
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_s
  = \!\left\{(x,y):\;
  x = \tfrac{L}{2}\,t,\quad
  y = A\sin\!\left(2\pi\nu x\right),\quad
  t\in[-1,1]
  \right\}\!.
  \label{eq:strip}
\end{equation}
Angles $\varphi$ are measured counterclockwise from $+x$. For the
infinite periodic continuation with period $\Lambda=1/\nu$,
normal-incidence Floquet theory gives the two angular branches
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \varphi_m^{\rm f} &= \arccos(m\nu),\\
    \varphi_m^{\rm b} &= 2\pi-\arccos(m\nu),
  \end{aligned}
  \qquad |m\nu|<1,
  \label{eq:floquet}
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_m^{\rm f}$ lies in the $+y$ forward (shadow-side)
hemisphere and $\varphi_m^{\rm b}$ lies in the $-y$ backward
(reflected) hemisphere. The infinite PEC continuation supports the
reflected branch; the finite strip additionally produces forward
endpoint diffraction. Its finite length ($L\nu$ periods) broadens the
periodic references into finite-strip lobes.
With $N=200$ nodes the worst-case arc length in the sweep
($A=2.5\lambda$, $\nu=0.30$) is $\approx32.31\lambda$, giving about
6.2 nodes per wavelength along the strip.
Near-field figures show the total field $U_0+U$ on a Cartesian grid,
normalized to the maximum intensity in the plotted window. Far-field
curves show $20\log_{10}|\Phi_{\rm sc}|$ normalized to the peak of the
corresponding pattern, so their purpose is angular comparison rather
than absolute calibration.

\section{Numerical Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Flat strip benchmark}

Setting $A=0$ gives a flat horizontal strip of length $L=10\lambda$.
The near-field and far-field benchmark plots are shown in
Figs.~\ref{fig:flat-near} and~\ref{fig:flat-far}; the boundary residual
of the $N=200$ discrete system is of order $10^{-13}$.
The reflected standing-wave region is below the strip, on the incident
side, whereas the shadow region is above it. This agrees with the
upward wave-vector arrow in Fig.~\ref{fig:flat-near}.
The far-field pattern exhibits the expected two-lobe structure:
the $90^\circ$ lobe corresponds to forward diffraction (shadow region),
and the $270^\circ$ lobe to backward specular reflection, with weaker
edge-diffraction contributions at $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$.

  from below along the $+y$ incident-wave direction.}
  \label{fig:flat-near}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!t]

Table~\ref{tab:convergence} shows $N$-convergence of $\sigma$.
The sequence rapidly approaches $40.0\lambda$, while the residual
remains near $10^{-13}$ or better. This residual measures algebraic
satisfaction of the discrete MDS system; convergence of the physical
observable is assessed by the change in $\sigma$.

Additional flat-strip checks at $N=400$ and $N=600$ changed the displayed
value only beyond the shown precision. Thus $N=200$ is used as the
working resolution for the sweep.

\subsection{Sinusoidal strip: finite-strip Floquet references}

For $A=1.5\lambda$, $\nu=0.20\,\lambda^{-1}$ ($L\nu=2$ periods)
the boundary residual is of order $10^{-13}$ and
$\sigma=40.011\lambda$.
The near-field in Fig.~\ref{fig:sin-near} shows that the corrugation
distorts the reflected standing-wave structure on the incident side
below the arc and produces a diffracted shadow-side field above it.
The corresponding far-field pattern is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sin-far}.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig3_sinusoidal_near.png}
  \caption{Sinusoidal strip ($A=1.5\lambda$, $\nu=0.20\,\lambda^{-1}$):
  normalized total near-field intensity with the strip profile overlaid.
  The arrow points from below along the $+y$ incident direction.}
  \label{fig:sin-near}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig4_sinusoidal_far.png}
  \caption{Sinusoidal strip normalized scattered far-field amplitude.
  Dotted markers show reflected $m\ne0$ Floquet references; dashed
  markers identify the forward and backward $m=0$ directions.}
  \label{fig:sin-far}
\end{figure}

The far-field in Fig.~\ref{fig:sin-far} reveals a forward finite-aperture
lobe at $90^\circ$ and strong reflected off-normal lobes near the
$m=-1$ and $m=+1$ references $258.5^\circ$ and $281.5^\circ$,
respectively. The computed maxima occur at approximately $258.2^\circ$
and $281.7^\circ$. Weaker $m=-2$ and $m=+2$ reflected signatures occur
near the $246.4^\circ$ and $293.6^\circ$ references. These angles are
consistent with the backward branch in~\eqref{eq:floquet} for
$\nu=0.20\,\lambda^{-1}$; finite length broadens and slightly shifts
the maxima. The corresponding forward first-order references at
$101.5^\circ$ and $78.5^\circ$ are much weaker and should not be
interpreted as the dominant reflected orders.

\subsection{Parametric pattern evolution}

Figures~\ref{fig:amp-sweep} and~\ref{fig:freq-sweep} summarize two
parametric sweeps over the reflected hemisphere
($180^\circ$--$360^\circ$).

\begin{figure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig5_amplitude_sweep.png}
  \caption{Reflected-hemisphere pattern evolution at fixed
  $\nu=0.20\,\lambda^{-1}$ as $A$ varies. Dotted verticals mark the
  backward $m=\pm1$ Floquet-reference angles.}
  \label{fig:amp-sweep}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig6_frequency_sweep.png}
  \caption{Reflected-hemisphere pattern evolution at fixed
  $A=1.5\lambda$ as $\nu$ varies. Dotted verticals mark the backward
  $m=\pm1$ reference angles for each spatial frequency.}
  \label{fig:freq-sweep}
\end{figure}

\emph{Amplitude dependence} [Fig.~\ref{fig:amp-sweep}]:
For the flat strip ($A=0$) the dominant reflected lobe sits at the
$270^\circ$ specular direction. Changing $A$ at fixed
$\nu=0.20\,\lambda^{-1}$ transfers relative amplitude among this lobe
and the off-normal reflected lobes near $258.5^\circ$ and $281.5^\circ$.
The dependence is not monotonic, but the first-order lobes become
especially prominent near $A=1.5$--$2.0\lambda$.

\emph{Frequency dependence} [Fig.~\ref{fig:freq-sweep}]:
With $A=1.5\lambda$ fixed, increasing $\nu$ moves the backward $m=+1$
reference from $275.7^\circ$ ($\nu=0.10$) to $287.5^\circ$
($\nu=0.30$), while the $m=-1$ reference moves from $264.3^\circ$ to
$252.5^\circ$, following~\eqref{eq:floquet}.
Because the finite strips contain only $L\nu=1$--3 periods, the observed
maxima are broad and can be shifted from the infinite-periodic markers.
The $m=\pm2$ references move farther from the specular direction as
$\nu$ increases and can organize additional weaker maxima.
Across all 25 configurations the total scattering width $\sigma$ spans
$38.940$--$40.656\lambda$; the range is $1.715\lambda$, or $4.3\%$ of
the mean value.
The smallest value occurs at $(A,\nu)=(2.0\lambda,0.25\lambda^{-1})$,
and the largest at $(2.5\lambda,0.20\lambda^{-1})$.
All sweep residuals remain of order $10^{-13}$ and below
$6\!\times\!10^{-13}$ in the current runs.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The flat-strip result separates the finite-aperture baseline from the
corrugation effects. For a horizontal PEC strip under normal incidence,
the dominant forward and backward lobes are set by aperture diffraction
and endpoint scattering. A sinusoidal profile mainly rephases the current
density along the same open conducting arc. Thus the integrated width
changes modestly in the sweep, while individual angular lobes can change
by many decibels.

The Floquet markers should also be read carefully. The forward branch
$\varphi_m^{\rm f}$ and reflected branch $\varphi_m^{\rm b}$ are exact
angles for an infinite periodic continuation, whereas the simulated
strip contains only $L\nu=1$--3 periods. The markers therefore organize
broad finite-aperture maxima rather than define strict finite-strip
diffraction orders. Endpoint contributions and local strip slope can
shift the actual peak positions.

The current implementation is restricted to the PEC Dirichlet model. It
therefore provides a clean reference case, while film thickness, kinetic
inductance, dielectric loading, and losses should be treated later
through impedance or transmission boundary conditions.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The MDS solver was applied to E-wave plane-wave scattering by
sinusoidal open PEC strips.
The solver was checked on a flat-strip benchmark: boundary residuals
remained near $10^{-13}$ and $\sigma$ converged to
$40.0\lambda$ by $N=200$.
For the sinusoidal strip, finite-strip far-field lobes were found near
the reflected infinite-periodic Floquet references
$\varphi_m^{\rm b}=2\pi-\arccos(m\nu)$, with finite-length broadening
and small peak shifts.
The parametric study identifies two independent design knobs: corrugation
amplitude $A$ controls the relative strength of off-normal lobes, and
spatial frequency $\nu$ sets the reflected Floquet-reference angles.
With the normalization used here, total scattering width is not invariant
in the tested range: it spans
$38.940$--$40.656\lambda$ across the sinusoidal sweep.
For superconducting meander-line devices these findings imply that the
corrugation profile controls both the far-field scattering signature and
the total scattered width, enabling shape-based crosstalk management in
densely integrated microwave circuits.
Planned extensions include H-wave polarization, oblique incidence, and
impedance boundary conditions for resistive or finite-conductivity layers.
